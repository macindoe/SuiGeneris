## Q1 (priority 1): the rule for exclusions about experience

The checkers' findings suggest a rule: **keep an exclusion about experience, consciousness or moral status as a `Paper:` clause, in the paper's own words with a locator, when the paper states it. When the paper does not, do not label it `Paper:`; rely on the fixed framework sentence where that sentence applies.** Cases the files name: Ferrara c01 (the paper says "They say nothing about consciousness, experience, or moral status, and we intend no such reading", §8, which GPT weakened to "does not establish the presence or absence"); Lindsey c07 (experience: paper-stated via footnote 12's definition of phenomenal consciousness and footnote 2; moral status: paper states uncertainty only); Macar c03 (the text never mentions moral status, welfare or "phenomenal"); Singh c01 to c06; the four prefill claims (the paper never discusses experience); Lederman c01 and c06 (the paper "takes no stand" on a narrower question than GPT's wording).

**Ask:** Is the rule right? Where does it fail, for example when a paper's silence is itself informative, or when no fixed sentence applies (the intervention-report claims in Ferrara and Macar carry neither)? Apply it to each case above and say what the field should do. Good answer: a verdict per case, a stated failure mode of the rule, and attention to whether the rule is symmetric. A paper's disclaimer is a `Paper:` clause; a library rule is not.

## Q5: verbs

Proposed rule: **a statement's verb names what was scored.** Use "reports detecting" where the scored event is the model's free-text report. Use "detects" or "flags" where the paper scores a forced-choice classification against a label and uses that word. Is that right? Apply it to Lederman c03 (the paper's results prose says "detect"; the scoring is a Haiku-graded classification of whether the response claims detection; the checker supports "report detecting") and to prefill c01 (the paper's abstract says "detects" and §3.2 "flags"; scoring is a forced "me / not me" answer against a tamper label). Note the checker's observation that prefill c06 is a different instrument (a 0 to 100 rating scored by AUROC). Good answer: a verdict per claim and whether the rule generalises to the Lindsey, Macar and Singh statements.
