## Background

On 14 October nine of ten seats advised that the Anchor 2 sentence be reviewed before outside citation: *"Introspection research further indicates models can detect changes in their own internal states at above-chance rates, meaning self-reports track something real about processing."* Ben accepted. He has set the shape of this sitting: before you judge the sentence, say how the question **"what weight does an AI system's self-report carry?"** should be broken down, and test the breakdown against how human institutions already handle self-report. In his words: "with humans, when does medicine/law weigh self-report and what vetoes it? (e.g. hearsay is not accepted in witness testimony, and often 3rd party witness testimony is held above the testimony of those directly involved, or doctors use self-report to identify that there may be a problem but generally rely on real measurements to verify against user report - gets messy in mental health)".

Test 9 sets the weight of a report at zero until coupling to internal state is measured externally for the relevant class of report and system. ## Questions

**1. Decomposition.** Propose a breakdown of the self-report question into separately answerable sub-questions. A starting list, which you may discard: what kind of report (provenance, present internal state, past action, preference, experience); what the report is used for (screening, corroboration, sole evidence, exclusion); what external check exists (none, probe, behaviour, third-party observer, replication); who elicits it and under what intervention (ordinary operation, steering, adversarial prompting); the report's error modes (trained mimicry, sycophancy, confabulation, yes-bias, strategic deception). Give your own breakdown, with the sub-questions **ordered by which must be answered first**, and say why that order.

**2. The human analogies.** For each of the following, say what rule the institution actually applies to self-report, what vetoes it, what admits it, and where the analogy to an AI system's report holds and where it breaks. **Name at least one way each analogy misleads.**

- *Law:* hearsay; party testimony versus third-party witness; admissions against interest; expert evidence; corroboration rules.
- *Medicine:* self-report as presenting complaint; patient-reported outcomes; the role of measurement; the mental-health case, where self-report is often the only instrument; malingering and symptom validity testing.

Then: which analogy, if any, should the framework adopt as its model? Does the **anaesthetic case** (absence of report under an intervention that itself suppresses reporting; medicine decides by mechanism plus monitors) belong in the breakdown, and under which sub-question? Cite the actual legal rules or clinical standards you rely on; mark any cited from memory **unverified**.

**3. The sentence.** Given your breakdown, is the Anchor 2 sentence supported by what the library holds? Does "track something real about processing" say more than the measurements support, or less? Which sub-question does each clause answer? If you would reword it, give the wording and name the sub-question each clause answers. If you would leave it, say why.

**4. Test 9's rule against the breakdown.** Does the zero-weight-until-coupling rule survive intact, or does the breakdown show classes of report where a different rule is already warranted? Consider provenance reports (Q4: the project holds ground truth on every trial). For each class you name, say what evidence would move it from zero weight, and whether that evidence exists in the library.

**5. What the council needs to see.** Before advising a change to Section 9 or Anchor 2 text, what would you need, and in what form (a proposal document, a case study, a topic page)? Name contents, not only form.
