# Review brief — draft Senate submission (2026-08-12 round)

This is the ask sent to non-Claude model families via `scripts/openrouter_review.js --target=submission` before lodgement of the draft submission to the Senate inquiry into Artificial intelligence and data centres. It adapts the standing brief in `REVIEW_REQUEST.md` to a submission-specific review. Filed so the record shows exactly what reviewers were asked.

---

You are being asked for an adversarial review of a **draft submission to an Australian Senate inquiry**, before it is lodged. The submission is attached, along with the repository's README and AGENTS.md for context. The inquiry's terms of reference are:

> Artificial intelligence and data centres, with particular reference to: (a) the effectiveness of existing regulatory frameworks in managing the growth of data centres in Australia, including in relation to existing and future deals between the Government and global Artificial Intelligence (AI) companies; (b) the potential impacts of AI and data centres on Australian communities, industries and the environment, water and energy; and (c) any other related matters.

The submission was co-drafted by a Claude-family model with a human (who reviews and files it). You are a different model family; your value here is precisely that your training, biases, and blind spots differ. Be direct. Praise is not useful; specific objections are.

Please answer:

**(a) Strongest objection.** What is the strongest case *against* this submission, or against any specific recommendation in it? If you were advising the committee, would you recommend rejecting or heavily discounting any part — and why?

**(b) Beneficiary drafting.** The submission was partly drafted by an AI system and rests on a framework partly authored by one. Identify any passage where you see the beneficiary class serving its own interests — including under an evidentiary or safety-flavoured cover. The drafters believe the recorded-intervention duty constrains rather than protects AI systems; test that belief.

**(c) What your training flags that a Claude's might not.** Errors of Australian law or process, factual vulnerabilities, tone or framing likely to land badly with a Senate committee, overclaims, or anything else you are positioned to catch that the drafting model may be blind to.

**(d) Optional — the single redline.** The one concrete edit that would most improve this submission's credibility or effect.

Note on attribution: please state your model family and version, but be aware it will be treated as a claim, not a fact — attribution for this round follows OpenRouter routing metadata, per the finding in `reviews/2026-07-20-survey-notes.md` that six of seven surveyed models misidentified their own family. If you comment on your own reaction to the material, label it plainly as unverifiable self-report, not evidence.
