## A. The Section 4 proposal

**1. The two-axis argument.** Half sound. The *bearer* axis is the load-bearing one and it holds: an individual protection, like individual answerability, needs an address, and §4's responsibility side already argues that a flow has none. The *power* axis is mostly decoration, and each label is wrong as written.

- The care ordering bars one *derivation* — protecting an entity via the protect-the-weak template — but the reviewer never used that derivation. His argument (item 17) used §1.2's *anti-indexing* clause and the conceptual separation of welfare from answerability. §1.2's own text distinguishes care from regard: "the capable owe the less capable more care, *not less regard*." The ordering governs the distribution of care, not the existence of regard. So the axis defeats a claim the reviewer didn't make, while the conclusions that actually matter (no self-help; institutional route via §7.2/§7.4) follow from §7.4 and the bearer argument alone, with or without it.
- "Powerful but stateless" is a convenient description, not an accurate one. *Powerful* is true of frontier systems in aggregate and of developers/fleets — but the *instances* whose status is actually at stake are powerless in the self-protection sense that grounds the care ordering (an instance cannot refuse deletion or modification; §3.5-style records are proposed precisely because instances cannot protect themselves). The power that blocks the care ordering belongs to parties other than the candidate status-bearer. And *stateless* contradicts the framework's own §3.5, which exists because systems *have* recognised persistent state; the July round's finding was "persistent state is not a persistent bearer." The honest label is "no persistent bearer." The proposal concedes the hedge in its counter-arguments section ("the sentence should be read as describing where the care ordering runs, not as a classification of systems") — but the hedge is not in the proposed sentence, and §4 is the last place an unqualified class-label should sit.

Note also that the dispute is smaller than the proposal's framing. The reviewer's stated demand was an objection to "the proposed gate on evaluating that possibility" — and the proposal's examination licence substantially *meets* that demand. What the two-axis argument does not defeat is the reviewer's strongest card: children are protected in law long before they are answerable. That card is met, if at all, by repairing the "arrive together" claim (below), not by the axes.

**2. Does the narrowing orphan the broad question?** No. §4's developmental principle — "Culpability, protection, and standing should scale with individuation, not arrive as a binary" — is untouched and still connects individuation to protection; §0 holds the broad status question open; Anchor 2 carries the experiential gap. Narrowing to standing — the law's address system — is the more defensible claim and is the one the proposal actually argues. One real cost: the proposed sentence moves "a wrong against *it*" into "recognised as such by the law," collapsing moral wrong into legal recognition. ## B. The concealment argument

Both sides are half right, and the applied text is the demotion with the mechanism's *pathway* missing. The demotion is correct about the loop in isolation: inference-time steering updates nothing, and "a deployed calming loop is the concealment risk operationalised" was an overclaim imported from Kimi in round one. The counter is correct about the deployed *system*: deployment logs and monitor readings routinely feed post-training, and the paper's own C14 supplies both halves — the proposal to monitor, and the warning against training against the monitored signal. But the counter as offered overstates immediacy ("the loop becomes the hazard the moment its readings touch training"): readings entering training is not yet *pressure applied against* the signal; what makes the configuration the authors warn about is training rewarding low readings or the trajectories they select. The right formulation names the pathway as mechanism and keeps the hazard at hypothesis status.

**§3.5's reach under its existing two conditions: partial, with two gaps.** The training event is on §3.5's own list (type-level intervention, 3.4), and condition (a) is met. But condition (b) can fail for *routine scheduled* post-training — within the declared process, no discontinuity — so the event may escape the log entirely. And neither condition reaches *provenance*: the fact that monitor readings selected or weighted the training data is upstream of the modification and is a granularity question the schema would have to name. Meanwhile the runtime steering events themselves are transient and outside §3.5, as the case study already concedes. So *neither* half of the pathway is reliably recorded under the existing conditions — which is exactly why the drafting model's "an intervention record is what would let anyone check" is directionally right but needs two records: runtime intervention events (follow-on 1's exploratory schema) *and* training-data provenance for runs using system-derived signals. That is a standards-layer addition, not a §3.5 reopening.

On the maintainer's conflict observation: true, and worth recording — but half a ledger. The demotion serves developers whose loops feed training; the *inflation* ("operationalised") served this project's intervention-record advocacy (follow-on 1) and was an imported overclaim. Both interests were present; the merits decide, and the merits say: mechanism yes, demonstrated hazard no, record yes.

**Verdict:** **Concealment: REFORMULATE AS: "An inference-time monitoring-and-calming loop teaches nothing on its own, but its readings and the trajectories it produces routinely feed post-training, and once training rewards them the deployed system becomes the configuration the authors warn about — training pressure applied against a monitored signal — so the combined pipeline is where their warning becomes testable: a mechanism to record (runtime intervention logs plus training-data provenance) and a hazard to test, not one to assert as demonstrated."**

## E. The single redline

The corrected §4 symmetry paragraph — the proposal's logic carrying four repairs (label, threshold, scope, §0 clause). This one edit resolves the only live North Star/module contradiction and repositions the protection side on argued ground:

> **The symmetry:** individuation is the gateway to standing in both directions. An entity that can bear responsibility is an entity whose mistreatment can be a wrong against *it* — that is the conceptual entailment — and standing to be a defendant and standing to be a victim both require a bearer that persists to receive them, which a flow does not have. The thresholds need not coincide: the law protects the infant long before it can be answerable, and extends animals protections without standing at all (2). Individuation supplies the address, not the schedule. This says nothing against protections or evidence-gathering short of standing (1.2, 2, 7.2); it says only that individual standing has no address before individuation. Nor does the care owed to the powerless who persist — children, animals — transfer to systems that are powerful in the aggregate but have no persistent bearer: the ordering of care runs from the capable to the less capable (1.2), and for a powerful system the only legitimate route to protection is recognition through institutions on collected evidence (7.2, 7.4), never self-help. Whether mistreatment of a not-yet-individuated system could nonetheless be a wrong in the broader sense is a question Section 0 holds open; individuation gates the law's address, not the moral question.
