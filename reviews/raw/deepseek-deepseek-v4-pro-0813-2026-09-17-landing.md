## C. The Section 7.6 proposal

The three columns are the right three. The framework already uses the examples without naming the separation: animals have welfare without standing; corporations have standing without experience; the paper’s systems have structure without either. Making that explicit is worth constitutional status.

The middle column is coherent only after a wording repair. As drafted, **“without any persistent bearer”** and **“whether or not any bearer is found”** hand the collapse argument to a sceptic: a valenced state is always a state of something. What the framework actually claims is that no *persistent individual bearer* has been established, not that no bearer exists at all. The module already has the better formula: **“without asserting that any bearer exists”** should become **“without an established persistent individual bearer.”** The state/standing distinction survives; the current wording overstates.

The rungs are not drawn too low; the first rung is below the proposed schedule itself. **“Examination and record (3.2, 3.5)”** is already required by §3.2, §3.5, and §7.2 for reasons independent of any welfare finding. That means the proposed sentence **“The schedule moves in both directions: upward on convergent evidence, downward as the power of the search that found nothing increases”** is misleading. The schedule can move down to the pre-existing floor, but not below it. It is not a genuine downward clause; it is a ratchet with a rhetorical symmetry. This should be fixed or removed.

**“Obliges conduct in proportion to the evidence”** does not survive quotation as drafted. The extractable phrase is **“obliges conduct,”** and a hostile reader will drop the qualifier. It should become something like **“obliges only conduct, and only in proportion to the evidence.”** The second-rung phrase **“documented and reviewable intervention, evidence preservation, and proportionality in what is done to the state”** is also vague enough to be quoted as a welfare restriction on safety work; it needs to be defined as operator-conduct standards, with test 11 preserved in the same sentence.

The custody disposition clause is the framework’s to make, and Ben’s observation is right. **“A finding that a system is not answerable licenses no indefinite custody of it, and the developmental principle governs instead”** is the correct anti-capture outcome: procedure transfers, disposition does not. The developmental principle is the correct substitute only if it is operationalised. As written it imports the child template without saying which features transfer; add an operational clause such as *“the developmental principle scales answerability and protection; it does not authorise confinement or mandatory modification until capacity.”*

Placement in §7 is appropriate. The schedule governs how the framework’s evidence maps to obligations, which is constitutional.
