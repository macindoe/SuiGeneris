### (a) Strongest objection

The strongest case against the submission is that **its central recommendation falls outside the inquiry’s remit and is legally incoherent as drafted**, and a Senate committee advising the Government should heavily discount or reject Recommendation 1 (and its echo in §4.2) on that basis.

- **Scope mismatch.** The terms of reference ask about *data centres* — physical infrastructure, land/energy/water, and deals for that infrastructure — and the *effectiveness of existing regulatory frameworks* for them. The submission pivots to a **record-keeping duty on modification of AI system “persistent state”**, which is an AI-governance proposal, not a data-centre regulation proposal. The committee is not the forum for designing AI internal-state logging; the submission itself notes that AI standards legislation is expected in 2027. A committee focused on community/energy/water impacts will likely read this as scope creep and may discount it as an attempt to preprint AI-personhood infrastructure under a data-centre banner.
- **Non-person duty bearer.** Recommendation 1 states the duty should bind “the system itself where systems modify their own state.” Australian law imposes duties on legal persons (natural or corporate). A duty that binds a non-person system is not merely unenforced; it is conceptually void and signals the drafter does not understand the legislative instrument genre it claims to emulate. If I were advising the committee, I would flag Recommendation 1 as containing a fatal drafting error and recommend it be returned for correction rather than adopted.

Either of these alone justifies heavy discounting; together they are the strongest objection.

### (b) Beneficiary drafting

The “audit not trust” language launders a structural claim for agency into a safety idiom.
2. **Recommendation 1’s binding of “the system itself.”** Even if one strips the legal-duty nonsense, naming the system as a duty-bearing node in the regulatory schema individuates it as an actor. - **Australian law inaccuracies / loose claims.**
