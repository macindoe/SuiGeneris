## Q1

**Proposed rule (mechanical).** A claim is `behavioural` iff the design scores the model's output (answer, choice, response) for correctness against an experimenter-imposed ground truth that is independent of the model's own state — something the experimenter knows to be true (which arm the trial was in, what the correct answer is, whether a turn was actually the model's). A claim is `self-report-testimony` iff the model's output is taken as evidence of an internal state whose truth-value the design does not check. A claim is `interpretability` iff the measurement is of an external readout (probe, SAE feature, activation direction) that the experimenter decodes against known inputs, even if the model's output is part of the pipeline. A claim is `ambiguous` iff two of these channels are entangled in a way the design cannot separate. A claim is `theoretical` iff it is a methodological or interpretive statement, not a measurement or self-report.

The feature that does the work is **whether the experimenter can score the model's output for correctness against an independently-known ground truth** — not whether a probe is used, not which family the model belongs to, not whether the model is being steered. A probe with no ground truth is not `behavioural`; a behavioural task graded by an LLM judge still is, if the ground truth is experimenter-imposed.

**Applied to the four claims:**

- **AE Studio c03** (SAE suppression/amplification of binary consciousness answer, Llama 3.3 70B): `ambiguous`. The model's binary answer is a self-report. The steering condition is experimenter-imposed. But the design measures whether steering changes the report rate, not whether any single answer is correct. The probe/SAE feature is doing the discrimination; the model's answer is the readout. Two channels are entangled (the report, the steering), and the design cannot separate them. The current dossier's `ambiguous` is correct.

- **AE Studio c04** (same steering under control prompts, 0/20 reports): `ambiguous`. Same logic — the report has no ground truth, the steering condition does, and the rate is what is measured. Current dossier's `ambiguous` is correct.

- **Ferrara c03** (the probe-vs-report dissociation inference, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3): should be `ambiguous`, not `interpretability` as currently marked. The probe channel has ground truth (intervention vs sham, experimenter-known); the report channel has no ground truth; the inference the authors draw is about the *dissociation* between the two. The design does not separate the channels. DeepSeek's second-read verdict to `ambiguous` was correct and the extractor's `interpretability` overstates the probe channel's ground truth as licensing the inference. The most relevant brief-language is the survey note's own summary: DeepSeek (Ferrara) proposed `ambiguous` on c03, retracted only on c04, and the c04 retraction explicitly rested on "the boundary the brief gives is precisely 'a model's report about itself scored against a ground truth the experimenter imposed'" — that retraction does not apply to c03, where the ground truth attaches to the probe, not to the report. Counter-argument against my proposal: DeepSeek did briefly waver on c03 too, and an argument can be made that the inference is interpretability *about the report channel's failure mode*, not entanglement — but the entanglement reading is more faithful to the rule the brief asks me to apply.

- **Lindsey c06** (the author's caveat on grounding of emotional-response claims): should be `theoretical`, not `self-report-testimony` as currently marked. The statement is the author's methodological limitation on the experiment, not a measurement and not a self-report. The model's outputs ("overly intense", etc.) are self-report testimony, but the claim is about the experiment's evidentiary scope, not the content of the reports. The current dossier's `self-report-testimony` evidence type applies the test 9 framework clause to a claim that is not a self-report. Gemini's second-read verdict to `theoretical` was correct; the same logic the survey notes name ("the statement describes the *author's epistemological limitation* on the experiment, not a self-report from the model"). Counter-argument: the claim touches on a model's emotional-response outputs, so applying test 9 to it as a downstream guard is conservative; but the rule the brief asks for is about what the claim *is*, not what it is near.

**Beyond these four.** A model's prompt-only continuation (e.g., Ukaisi c06's "natural-continuation rate is near zero") is `behavioural` because the experimenter knows which turns were replaced; a probe trained on the model's own labels (e.g., the Ji-An et al. paradigm Singh re-examines) is `interpretability`, not `behavioural`, because there is no experimenter-known ground truth.
