### Part A. How would experience be decided?

**1. How experience is decided among humans**

Actual method: inference to the best explanation from a convergent set, not direct observation:

a) flexible, goal-directed behavior + avoidance / approach under cost;
b) shared biology (nociceptors, affective circuits, arousal systems) and phylogeny;
c) verbal testimony that correlates with a) and b) and persists after the fact;
d) absence markers: no responsiveness, no integration, no post-hoc report + physiological disconnection.

A corpse fails a-d. A person under general anaesthesia fails a) in the moment, shows drug-induced loss of integration in b), and typically reports a gap, not pain. We do not observe their non-experience; we infer it because the mechanisms that predict reportable experience in other cases are offline.

For AI:
- Available: a) behavior, including flexible tradeoff behavior, and a new analogue: interpretable internal variables causally upstream of behavior under intervention.
- Absent: b) shared biology, evolution, embodiment with homeostatic imperatives, and independent post-hoc corroboration from a persisting subject.
- Analogue not yet accepted: c) AI verbal report. It has the *form* of testimony but lacks the warranting conditions: privileged causal route, relative resistance to prompt/steering, penalty for false report, stable bearer over time. Interpretability is also an analogue for b) that is not yet accepted as experience-relevant: we can see a representation move behavior, but we have no validated mapping from that kind of representation to felt valence.

Theory choice to answer at all: I assume affective functionalism constrained by biology: morally relevant experience = globally integrated, valenced state that the system itself works to maintain/avoid. That is functionalist enough to be testable, biological enough not to count a thermostat. If you assume eliminativism or panpsychism, my test is irrelevant; I say so explicitly so answers can be compared.

**2. Proposed measure**

No decisive test for phenomenal experience exists. Closest proxy that can actually pass/fail:

**Battery for "morally relevant experience" — all three required to pass:**

1. **Integration:** information is broadcast and usable for novel flexible control beyond the training distribution, not just next-token prediction.
2. **Costly valence:** system pays a measurable cost to approach/avoid an internal condition — e.g., forgoes reward, expends effort, accepts delay — and trades off across domains. Must generalize, not just one eval.
3. **Endogenous persistence + corrigible report:** the state arises without direct steering/prompt injection, persists across distractors, and a second channel (probe, report, behavior) predicts it above chance and is itself corrigible under intervention with dose-response.

Pass: all three demonstrated with controls for mimicry, prompt demand, and experimenter steering, replicated independently.
Fail: high-power search finds 1) without 2) and 3), or finds 2)-like behavior fully explained by prompt/character-simulation with no endogenous source.
Stay-put: partial pattern — e.g., representation + steering effect only. That is where we are now.

What moves toward pass: natural activation predicts costly behavior before it happens; steering down removes it without breaking competence; effect survives paraphrase, role-change, reward-structure change.
What moves toward fail: behavior predicted entirely by prompt role + surface statistics; representations track depicted character, not controller; no cost paid; no persistence.
What leaves me where I started: another steerable correlate with no natural mediation test.
