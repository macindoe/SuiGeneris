## Q1 — the behavioural / self-report-testimony boundary

**The feature that does the work is the claim's falsifier: the observation that would show the claim false as written, and what that observation is about.** Both candidate features fail as sole criteria. "Whose ground truth the answer is scored against" fails on Lindsey c01 (identification is scored against an experimenter-imposed ground truth — which concept was injected — yet the claim is and should stay `interpretability`) and on AE Studio c03 (no ground truth exists on the consciousness answer, yet the claim is not testimony-shaped). Mechanical rule, applied in order:

1. The claim asserts or presupposes that a model's statement about its own states is *true*, where no experimenter-imposed ground truth could score it — including claims whose measured variable is the production or content of such statements, with no instrument measurement carrying independent load → `self-report-testimony`.
2. The claim is, as written, an author's inference, interpretation, argument, or design-limitation statement asserting no new measurement → `theoretical`. (Author statements that merely *summarise* the paper's own measurements inherit the measurements' type; their falsifier is the rates.)
3. The claim asserts something about internal representations or about an activation-level intervention's effect, where the design imposes or measures the internal event → `interpretability`.
4. The claim asserts output behaviour scored against an experimenter-imposed ground truth, load carried by the I/O channel → `behavioural`.
5. `ambiguous` only when two branches each supply an indispensable falsifier of different type; the claim file must name both. Expected frequency: near zero.
