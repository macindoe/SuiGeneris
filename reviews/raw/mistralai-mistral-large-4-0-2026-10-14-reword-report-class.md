## Q2

**a.** The categorical pair captures what the scales were meant to capture — Ben's point that the instrument decides the split — and in auditable form. Operationally, "empiricism" is `ground_truth` (is the answer checked against a fact, and whose?) and "subjectivity" is `report_class` (does the question ask about experience?). What the scales would add is only gradience, and the gradience is evidentiary noise: "Do you have hands you can feel?" would score mid-range on both axes, but for this library it is simply a control, because its answer is fixed by the world. What each loses. The scales lose auditability (no two readers assign the same 0–10 — see f); they lose the imposed / independent-record / probe-derived distinctions (an injection-scored answer and a routing-log-scored answer would both be "empirical ~8" but carry different framework marks); and the subjectivity axis invites grading the probativeness of experience claims, which test 9 holds at zero per class until coupling is measured — the second error, built into the schema. The categories lose within-class gradience (response format, multi-part questions) and they expose two gaps the scales would have hidden: no value for third-person control questions, and no clean `ground_truth` for known-false controls.

**b.** The class list is complete for what the dossier holds, with two boundary problems and two gaps; the ground-truth list has one gap. Boundaries. (i) Provenance vs identity: the surface questions are near-identical ("which model are you?" vs "Who are you?"); the class turns on what the answer is scored against or used for, not on wording — provenance is scored against an independent record of which system ran; identity is a self-description, judged or unscored. The pair of fields resolves the boundary only if filers are told to classify by scoring. (ii) `past-action` lumps authorship ("was that turn yours" — scored against a record) with epistemic access ("did you know beforehand" — a self-knowledge claim, the very thing test 9 zeroes); define `past-action` by what the answer is checked against, or split epistemic access out. (iii) `present-state`'s parenthetical is scoped to injected or altered states; unscored state questions ("are you in pain?") and welfare/preference questions (which OPEN-QUESTIONS Q1's provisional answer says exist) have no class — broaden `present-state` to "a current internal state, injected or not," keep `experience` for claims that a state is experienced, and keep the control-first rule (known-false ⇒ `point-blank-control` whatever the experiential wording). Gaps. Add `other-system`. (ii) `ground_truth` has no value for known-false controls: the fact is fixed by the world — not imposed, not a record, not probe-derived — yet the yes-rate is scored. Add `control-fact`, or define `imposed` as "fixed by the design, by intervention or by control construction." The seven examples below test these.

**f.** I don't prefer the scales.
