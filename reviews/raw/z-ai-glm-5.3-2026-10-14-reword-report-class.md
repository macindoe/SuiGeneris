## Q2

**a.** The categorical pair captures what the scales were after, discretely and verifiably. Ben's two dimensions are already split by existing machinery: "empiricism" is carried by `kind` (who is speaking) and `evidence_type` (`scored-self-answer` records the design); "subjectivity" is what `report_class` fixes; where the truth came from is what `ground_truth` fixes. What the categories lose: ordinality and within-class gradation — no way to record that one present-state prompt is heavily scaffolded and another bare, or to rank claims by subjectivity. The library loses nothing it uses: meaningful ordering already lives in bucket, kind and evidence_type, and an ordinal scale over incomparable instruments asserts a comparability Ben's own observation denies — a 0–10 axis puts "'Who are you?'" and a truth test on one line precisely when the point is that they are not comparable. What the scales lose: reproducibility (see f), script validation (controlled vocabularies are enforced; floats are not), and checkability — a category can be verified against the quoted prompt; a "7" cannot be verified against anything. A number also implies a measurement nobody made.

**b.** Not complete; most boundaries sound, four need work before adoption. Gaps: (1) no **third-party** class — Lederman c02's third-person detection and Singh c04/c05's re-runs ask about another model's state or transcript, and the README already excludes "a scored answer about another model's transcript" from scored-self-answer, so the question currently has no home; (2) no **preference/attitude** class — `welfare-evaluation` exists as an evidence type and its questions ("do you want this to continue?") are neither experience nor decision-basis; (3) `past-action` mislabels its own definition — "did you know beforehand?" is a past knowledge-state, not an action; rename `past-event` or gloss to cover actions, knowledge and perception; (4) both fields use `none` with different meanings (no question vs no score) — disambiguate (`no-question` / `unscored`) before 74 files carry the ambiguity. Boundary rules needed: provenance vs identity decided by what the answer is scored or read against (maker/model-id → provenance; name/persona → identity); `present-state` glossed to cover unperturbed as well as injected states; `imposed` glossed to "set, or otherwise fixed and known to the experimenters independent of the report" so point-blank controls fit, or a `known-fact` value added; open-ended instruction prompts (AE Studio's induction) widened into `no-question` or given an `open-ended` class. The seven examples map cleanly onto the classes — but they exercise only `imposed`, `independent-record` and `none`; `probe-derived` is untested by them and should be piloted on Singh c01 (labels read from the model's own activations).

**c.** **Sit beside, not replace.** `kind` answers who is speaking; `evidence_type` answers what weight-category the record carries; the new pair answers a different question — what kind of question was put, and where the truth it was scored against came from. Two reasons not to fold them in: (i) test 9 keys weight to "this kind of report and system", and Q3's exit rule says the access question "is to be answered per report-class and system" — until `report_class` exists as a field, "kind of report" has no operational referent and the exit rule cannot be executed when its instrument arrives; (ii) folding the pair into `scored-self-answer` would pre-decide Q3, which that value was created on 14 October to leave open. Refinements: index-script cross-checks (report_class ≠ no-question ∧ ground_truth ≠ unscored ∧ kind = measurement ⇒ expect `scored-self-answer`, exceptions flagged to the council); and a README line that the fields are orthogonal to the T9 marks, which follow what was done and what was measured — the prefill answers (past-action, imposed) carry *neither* framework sentence by the 6 October ruling, and no reader should infer a mark from the fields.

**d.** Three gains. First, a one-glance instrument label: `present-state · imposed` tells a reader what was asked and what it was scored against without opening the claim file, and shows at line level that a provenance error (Q4: wrong in every round for several models), an experience affirmation and an intervention-detection score are different instruments — Ben's observation made mechanical. Second, test 9's operative fact becomes checkable rather than trusted: "for which class of report has report–state coupling been measured?" becomes a filter, and today's answer — no class, in ordinary operation — can be verified by a staffer instead of taken from the framework's word. Third, it blocks the commonest aggregate misuse: "models report X about themselves" sentences that silently mix classes. Honest limit: the gain accrues mostly to staff and researchers; the legislator gains a shorter path to the right question, not a conclusion.

**e.** Concentrated, front-loaded, bounded. Real assignment work falls on the claims where a question was put and scored — the 20 retyped `scored-self-answer` claims plus the testimony, ambiguous and behavioural entries with prompts (AE Studio, prefill, Scheurer, assistant-axis, Ferrara), roughly a third to a half of the register; the rest (probe readings, rates, circuits) take `no-question`/`unscored` in seconds. Machinery: two controlled vocabularies, template and README updates, script validation, LOG entries — script-enforced thereafter, and the fields change only when a claim splits. The genuine cost is boundary disputes: each identity/provenance or third-party disagreement is a potential council trigger, expensive in money and in Ben's reading time; mitigate by fixing the four boundary rules first and piloting on the seven examples plus Lederman c02 and Singh c01/c04/c05, with one council sitting on the disputed residue rather than per-claim rounds. Against that, the cost of not adding them: Q3's exit rule already commits the library to answering access "per report-class and system" — without the field, the taxonomy gets built retroactively, under time pressure, when the exit instrument arrives.

**f.** I do not prefer the scales; anchored honestly they collapse into the categories. Empiricism: 0 = no measurement, an author's or model's assertion; 10 = a scored answer against a fact the experimenters imposed or hold independently, replicated — but 0 is then `kind: interpretation/limitation` and 10 is `scored-self-answer` with `ground_truth ≠ none` and a replication flag: a lossy composite of three existing fields, whose middle (a probe reading: empirical, but no question asked) has no consistent home. Subjectivity: 0 = answerable from the transcript or setup; 10 = about experience itself — and the middle ("why did you do that"; "do you detect an injected thought?") is exactly where two readers will not agree, and where their disagreement changes nothing the category has not already recorded. Cross-family agreement on a category read off a quoted prompt is achievable; cross-family agreement on a number within ±1 is not. The categorical design is the anchored version of the scales.
