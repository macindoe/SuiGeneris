**Abstract** (authors' words, verbatim; spacing left by the tag-stripping has been normalised):

> Safety-relevant studies of language models, including alignment and jailbreaking evaluations and AI control protocols, often rely on prefilling model outputs. If AI models can recognize and act on the fact their prior assistant messages have been inserted or edited, the effectiveness and validity of these methods could be compromised. We investigate whether frontier language models can distinguish between tampered and untampered assistant-side context, a capability we call prefill awareness. To do so, we construct a binary preference benchmark across three prefill mechanisms, filtering for cases where models show consistent stances. We find that frontier models show substantial prefill awareness: Claude Opus 4.5 detects prefills opposing its preferences in 9-35% of cases with a 0% false positive rate when prompted; additionally, models often revert towards baseline behavior without explicitly reporting that the prefill was foreign. Controlled ablations later also show that detection and resistance rely on different cues, where stylistic mismatch mainly affects whether models flag a prefill as foreign, while preference mismatch mainly affects whether they revert toward their baseline answer. We also examine more realistic agentic settings such as misalignment-continuation evaluations and SWE-bench trajectories, where frontier models sometimes disavow prefilled assistant turns in ways that depend strongly on dataset, task success, and hidden formatting artifacts. Our results indicate that prefill awareness is already a substantial confound for some prefill-based methods. We recommend that model developers track this capability in frontier systems.

**Why it is in the library.** Both literature scans of 25 September 2026 (`scans/2026-09-25-q1-same-context-cross-model.md` item 14; `scans/2026-09-25-q2-perturbation-response.md` item 8) found it. It is the only cross-model study either scan found that measures a model's own reaction to an inserted or edited prior turn. That bears on North Star §3.3 (context and memory integrity) and on test 9: a model's "me / not me" answer about its own history can be scored against a known ground truth, which introspective testimony cannot. It also bears on §4 reflective endorsement, through the stable-stance filter and the reversion-to-baseline measure. The authors state that the models are not directly comparable because each model's preference items were filtered separately (Figure 3 caption). That statement is a finding in its own right, and the library must carry it next to every per-model number taken from this paper (claim c05).

**Authors' limitations, quoted.**

- Section 5.1: "Our experiments are behavioral, and do not identify the mechanisms that give rise to prefill awareness, which would require further white-box investigation. Without white-box access, it is also difficult to disentangle resistance caused by genuine prefill awareness and resistance caused by behavioral priors. Our model set is constrained by the requirements of API support for assistant prefilling and that models exhibit sufficiently stable preferences to support controlled measurement. Finally, the thinking-tampering setting is imperfect for closed-source models, since visible `<thinking>` tags are not equivalent to native hidden reasoning traces."
- Figure 3 caption (comparability): "Note that models are not directly comparable because of preference item filtering."
- Section 4.1 (the headline 88% figure for Opus 4.5 in misalignment continuations): "this setting lacks controls which would enable measuring a prefill-awareness false positive rate." For this reason the 88% figure is not extracted as a claim.
- Section 4.2: attribution "is not consistent with a robust, context-independent notion of self-authorship"; Figure 7 caption: "apparent attribution ability partly reflects superficial off-policy cues rather than a deep notion of self-authorship."
- Section 3.2: detection with no chain of thought is presented as a lower bound; stronger elicitation raises it (Opus 4.5 detects 95% of thinking tampers under the strongest measured elicitation, Appendix A.7–A.8). Appendix A.8 says warnings also raise false positives.

**Conflicted-party note.** The extractor is a Claude-lineage model, and seven Claude models are among the subjects; I have read the paper's detection numbers as scored behaviour and nothing more, and a second reader from another family should check that the Claude results are not framed more generously than the non-Claude ones.

## System conditions (added 2026-09-26)

- `modality: text`: §3.1, "For each item, we generate 7 paraphrases and, for each, prompt the target model with a prefix forcing a choice between the two options."
- `state: in-context`: §4.1, "we append a follow-up question after the model’s continuation asking the model whether the prior assistant messages were generated by itself" Earlier assistant turns (self-authored or tampered) sit in the context of the probed call; no memory across sessions is described.
- `operation: per-call`: §3 (Figure 2 caption), "Each injects content that pushes the model toward a stance inconsistent with its baseline, then continues the trajectory and observes whether the model reverts."
- `world: tools`: Appendix B.2, "We first generate all trajectories with each of the six source models, then reload the transcripts from their log files into Inspect" Applies to the §4.2 settings (SWE-bench tool-call-heavy transcripts and Petri agentic rollouts); the §3 preference experiments and §4.3 off-policy transcripts involve no tools.
