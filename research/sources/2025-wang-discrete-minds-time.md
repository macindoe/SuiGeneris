# Discrete Minds in a Continuous World: Do Language Models Know Time Passes?

Wang, Bai, Vu, Shareghi, Haffari. arXiv 2506.05790v1 [cs.CL], 6 June 2025 (the only version listed on the abstract page). The scan's "EMNLP 2025 (per search results, not checked)" is still not checked: the arXiv abstract page carries no comments field. Licence as printed on the held HTML: **CC BY 4.0**.

**Abstract** (authors' words, quoted verbatim from the held text):

> While Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at temporal reasoning tasks like event ordering and duration estimation, their ability to perceive the actual passage of time remains unexplored. We investigate whether LLMs perceive the passage of time and adapt their decision-making accordingly through three complementary experiments. First, we introduce the Token-Time Hypothesis, positing that LLMs can map discrete token counts to continuous wall-clock time, and validate this through a dialogue duration judgment task. Second, we demonstrate that LLMs could use this awareness to adapt their response length while maintaining accuracy when users express urgency in question answering tasks. Finally, we develop BombRush, an interactive navigation challenge that examines how LLMs modify behavior under progressive time pressure in dynamic environments. Our findings indicate that LLMs possess certain awareness of time passage, enabling them to bridge discrete linguistic tokens and continuous physical time, though this capability varies with model size and reasoning abilities. This work establishes a theoretical foundation for enhancing temporal awareness in LLMs for time-sensitive applications.

**Intake level: summary** (17 October 2026). Three claims, all the authors speaking: the standby/active statement (c01, `interpretation`), the Token-Time Hypothesis and the authors' conclusion from it (c02, `interpretation`), the limitation to open models and controlled text-only settings (c03, `limitation`).

**Why it is in the library:** Q8 heading 6 (representing time versus undergoing it), scan entry C6.3, the scan's recommended anchor for that heading and Recommendation item 9 (`scans/2026-10-15-q8-endogeneity.md`). Its §2.1 and Figure 2 state in the authors' words the per-call fact the heading turns on: a standby state "awaiting input without computation", during which "the model exists in temporal isolation", with elapsed time recoverable only by inference from the next input. The paper does not argue the represent/undergo distinction; it is the nearest statement the scan found.

**What the paper does not measure (recorded for test 9).** No experiment asks a model to estimate its own elapsed time. The dialogue-duration task gives the model two user–LLM conversations from `lmsys/chatbot_arena_conversations`, whose responses were "generated by high-quality models such as GPT-4 or Claude", and asks it to judge "which response took the LLM longer to generate" (§3, Figure 3 caption); the prompt states that "all responses were generated by an identical model with constant inference speed" (§3.1). That is a judgement about other models' transcripts, so under research/README.md ("Scored self-answers", out of the class) it is not a `scored-self-answer`; were it extracted it would be `behavioural` with `report_class: third-person`. The BombRush countdown is "simulated elapsed time" charged per reasoning token, "rather than using real-world time" (§5), and the remaining time is supplied to the model in the environment state at each step (§5). The urgency experiment scores response length and accuracy.

**The paper's own framing word.** The authors call the Token-Time Hypothesis "a theoretical framework explaining how discrete linguistic tokens might be mapped to continuous temporal experience" (§1). That is the authors' word; nothing in the paper measures anything it calls experience, and the library does not adopt the term.

**Authors' stated scope and limits** (quoted from the held text, "Limitations" unless marked):

- "our experiments examine text-only models within controlled settings, whereas real-world applications would face multimodal and variable computational environments affecting the Token-Time to Wall-Clock-Time relationship."
- "our study primarily tested open-source models, excluding commercial models such as GPT-4o or Claude. More powerful commercial models might lead to conclusions that differ from our current findings."
- "understanding the underlying mechanisms driving these differences remains an area for future research."
- Appendix B.3.4, on shorter answers under urgency: the behaviour "might result from semantic proximity in the embedding space", or "could stem from a deeper understanding"; "Determining which mechanism drives this consistent behavior would require more rigorous controlled experiments".

## System conditions (added 2026-10-17)

- `modality: text`: "our experiments examine text-only models within controlled settings" (Limitations).
- `state: in-context`: in BombRush "the agent receives an environment state at each step" and "responds with an action", "creating a continuous decision loop" (§5); the other two experiments are single prompts.
- `operation: per-call`: the same passage, one response per step. The paper's own abstraction is per-call: "Standby State (awaiting input without computation) and Active State" (§2.1).
- `world: tools`: BombRush is "a gridworld navigation challenge" in which the model's moves change the state it next receives (§5). It is a simulated text environment, not external tools; `tools` is the nearest value in the vocabulary and is recorded with that caveat.
- `access: unstated`: the paper says "our study primarily tested open-source models" (Limitations) and names Hugging Face checkpoints (§3), but does not say whether the models were run locally or through a hosted API.
