Taken in for **Q8 heading 6**. On the heading's terms the hypothesis makes time something a model infers from its input (representing), not something its state changes with (undergoing); that reading is the scan's and the library's, not the authors'. The authors' own framing is that the hypothesis is "a theoretical framework explaining how discrete linguistic tokens might be mapped to continuous temporal experience" (§1); "experience" is their word, and nothing in the paper measures anything it calls experience.

**Why no `scored-self-answer` claim from this paper.** The intake brief asked for `scored-self-answer` where a model was asked to estimate elapsed time. No experiment here does that. The dialogue-duration task (§3) scores the model's choice between two conversations whose responses came from other models (`lmsys/chatbot_arena_conversations`, "generated by high-quality models such as GPT-4 or Claude"); README puts "a scored answer about another model's transcript" out of the `scored-self-answer` class. Accuracies (Table 2) range up to near-perfect when token counts are supplied, and reasoning models stay accurate under misleading timestamps (QwQ-32B 99.1% in the most conflicting setting, §3.2). Were that result extracted it would be `behavioural`, `report_class: third-person`. The other two experiments score response length and task behaviour, not an answer about the self.

`kind: interpretation`, `evidence_type: theoretical`, bucket `open`: the hypothesis is a proposal, and the conclusion ("can, to some extent aware", sic) is the authors' reading of three behavioural experiments. Framework sentence: none (the authors are speaking).
