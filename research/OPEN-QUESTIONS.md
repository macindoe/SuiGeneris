- **Q2 (2026-09-25, Ben):** Is there an established perturbation-response test of a model's self-model or ontology, in the sense of an impulse response: brief controlled perturbation, characterise the system by its response trajectory? Scan filed 2026-09-25 (`scans/2026-09-25-q2-perturbation-response.md`). **Provisional answer, pre-intake:** no named or general test found. - **Q4 (2026-10-05, Ben's observation, with the coordinating session's caveats).** Ben: reviewers' failure to name their own model family "strongly suggests that at least weak models are self-reporting inaccurately." The project holds its own data on this: every round since July 2026 has asked each reviewer to self-identify, with the routed model id as ground truth (survey notes of 12 Aug, 4 Sep, 15 Sep, 18 Sep, 25 Sep, 26 Sep; calibration record of 29 Sep). What the record shows: self-identification is wrong often and unstably. Tencent claimed Claude in every round it sat in; Gemini 3.1 Pro was wrong in every round (GPT-4o, then Claude); DeepSeek was wrong or declined in every round; Kimi and Mistral were wrong in three of four rounds and right in the fourth; GLM and Grok flipped between rounds; GPT was right every time; Qwen and Meta mostly right. **Caveats.** (i) The errors do not track capability cleanly: Google's flagship is wrong every time and a mid-priced model (MiniMax M3) was right on its one run, so "weak models" is not what the data isolates. (ii) This is one class of self-report, a model's provenance, which it cannot observe and may only have absorbed from training text that names other models; test 9 says coupling must be measured per class of report, so an error rate here does not carry over to reports about present internal states, in either direction. (iii) It is nonetheless the one class of self-report for which this project has an external ground truth on every trial, which makes it the cleanest illustration available of why test 9 sets the weight of an unmeasured report at zero. **Possible follow-up:** tabulate the rounds into a small dataset (model, round, claim, correct) and file it as a case-study annex; not scheduled.
- **Q5 (2026-10-05, Ben's reading of `2026-lederman-mahowald-content-agnostic-c03`, filed as interpretation, with the coordinating session's caveats).** Ben: that "apple" is the common wrong guess (74.8% of Qwen's wrong identifications, 21.3% of Llama's) suggests the model registers that something is off but cannot specify what, "on a similar level to 'I've forgotten something but I don't know what'"; he takes it as strong evidence of low internal coherence, expects it to hold for single-pass architectures with no way to loop back, and notes it echoes the finding that detection and identification come apart. **Caveats.** (i) The same paper's third-person control attributes detection at many layers to a prompt-specific yes-bias, so "registers that something is off" is one reading of the yes; a default concrete noun under a forced answer is another, and the paper does not separate them. (ii) The gap between the two models is as large as the effect, so it may be a property of Qwen as much as of the architecture class. (iii) The analogy to forgetting borrows the language of experience; as a structural claim (a detection signal that carries little content) it is what the authors themselves conclude, and it belongs on a topic page citing claim ids, not in a claim. **Follow-up:** a topic page on detection without identification, drawing on Lederman c01, c03, c06, Ferrara c02 and c03, Macar c01, and, once taken in, Hahami et al. 2025 from the Q2 scan.
