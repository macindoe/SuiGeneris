## The prompt

You are scanning the literature for a research library attached to a legal framework (`north-star-sui-generis-ai-category.md`). Read `research/README.md` first: the library's units are sources, claims and dossiers; a scan is a pre-intake docket, filed verbatim, never cited. Then read Q8 in `research/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` and §4 (Conditions of Individuation), §1.1, §5 Anchor 3, §7.5 and §9 test 11 of the North Star. Also read Q2 and Q7 in OPEN-QUESTIONS and the Q2 scan's closing section ("Is there a named test?"): Q8 is the same question from a third side.

**The question.** Call a system *endogenous* to the degree that something happens in it without an input. Two functional conditions are proposed, and the scan should test whether the literature already has better ones: (a) the system's state evolves between inputs; (b) that evolution can itself produce output or action. The human brain satisfies both (intrinsic activity, spontaneous thought, self-initiated movement). A transformer served per call satisfies neither: no forward pass runs without a prompt, and when generation stops the system does not resume. A scheduled "heartbeat" delivers a token sequence, so the clock is external and symbolic. Within a run, though, a transformer generates content that was not in its input (reasoning text; every token it feeds back to itself), so the gap is in *initiation*, not in content generation. The framework must state any such property functionally (§7.5): "no forward pass between calls" is a fact about one generation of serving infrastructure.

**Find, for each of the following, the three to five papers a careful reviewer would expect, with the single best anchor named:**

1. **Intrinsic activity in the brain.** The default-mode and resting-state literature; spontaneous thought and mind-wandering; the argument that the brain is intrinsically active and inputs modulate rather than drive it; the mechanisms that sustain activity without input (pacemaker cells, recurrent loops, noise-driven excitability). What fraction of activity is intrinsic, by whose measure, and what the controversies are.
2. **Self-initiated versus externally triggered action in humans.** The experimental literature that separates the two (self-paced versus cued movement; readiness potentials and their critics; the "volition" literature). Which measure separates initiation from response, and whether it has been applied to anything artificial.
3. **Artificial systems whose state evolves in continuous time or between inputs.** Continuous-time recurrent networks, neural ordinary differential equations, liquid time-constant networks, spiking networks, reservoir computing, Hopfield and attractor networks. For each: does the state evolve without input, and does anything couple that evolution to output? Separately, state-space and recurrent language models (which carry state but, as served, step only on input), and any "always-on", "idle-time", test-time-training or continual-learning design in which a language model computes between user turns.
4. **Agents that act without a prompt.** Active-inference agents; reinforcement-learning agents with intrinsic motivation or curiosity; any evaluation of a language-model agent that measures what it does when given no task. Distinguish initiation by design (a loop the developer wrote) from initiation the authors treat as a property of the model.
5. **Measurement.** Has anyone, in either field, defined or measured initiation as distinct from response? Has anyone proposed a test that a scaffolded system (a cron loop around a per-call model) would *fail* and an intrinsically active system would pass? Does noise count: has anyone separated intrinsic from stochastic activity, in brains or in sampled models?
6. **Representing time versus undergoing it.** Work on temporal features in language-model activations (elapsed time, dates, durations), time-perception tasks given to language models, and any argument distinguishing a system that represents time from one whose state changes with it.
7. **Spontaneous resistance to instructions.** Ben's first question: is there any documented case of a current system declining or deviating from an instruction with no identifiable conflict (no trained value, system prompt or task goal that the instruction crossed)? List the resistance cases the literature does document (refusals, alignment faking, shutdown resistance, agentic misalignment, implicit reversion after tampering) and say, for each, what the conflict was. If every case is a conflict case, say so plainly; that is a finding.

**Method, as the Q6 scan set it.** Download every abstract page and, where quoted, the full text with `curl`; strip tags locally with a uniquely named script kept apart from the downloaded files; search the stripped text by hand. Do not use a summarising fetch tool for any quotation. Every quotation must be your own reading of the downloaded text. Where you could not download a paper, list it under "Not verified" with what you searched. Record for each entry: authors, date, venue or arXiv id, URL, retrievability, models or organisms studied, what it bears on (which of 1 to 7, and which North Star section), the finding in the authors' words, the limits the authors state, the publisher relation (developer of studied model, independent, government, mixed; say when inferred), and a verification line (abstract read; full text read, searched: sections). Note any title or date discrepancy between search results and the fetched page. Keep a "Corrections and cautions found during the scan" section at the top, a "Gaps" section, a "Not verified" section, and a "Recommendation" naming which papers to take in and at which tier (full or summary level), with an intake effort estimate. Record the search queries you ran.

**Output.** One markdown file, to be filed verbatim as `research/scans/2026-MM-DD-q8-endogeneity.md` with the standard header ("Status: scan, not intake", the question in Ben's words, your method sentence, the conflict disclosure above). Do not propose edits to the North Star. Do not use the word "experience" for anything a paper measures unless the paper does; where a paper does, quote it.
