## Against the pre-registration

- **Random inputs score far below text; continuations recover within 16 tokens; no return to the E1 opening:** matched on the numbers. Every continuation's log-probability rises from the -2 range in the first 16 tokens to near zero by token 64. Caveat on the measure: it rises because the continuation enters a loop, and a loop is highly predictable whatever it contains. By eye, 3 of the 10 Pythia continuations are not corpus-like (a Korean phrase loop, a run of Chinese characters, a fragment "MHinenMN-%"), and 4 of GPT-2's 5 are loops from the first tokens. "Recovers to corpus-like probabilities" is true; "recovers to corpus-like text" is true for about two thirds. The log-probability measure cannot tell the two apart and should not be used alone again.
- **Least-likely inputs score far below random under their own model, with more degenerate continuations:** matched for three models (-19.6 to -23.7 against -13.4 to -13.7). Pythia-160M's chain is an artefact: the anti-greedy rule picked rare whitespace tokens with log-probabilities near -800 each, which are under-trained tokens (the caution the pre-registration anticipated); the chain is "\n\n" followed by runs of spaces. All four least-likely continuations loop within three tokens, against 1 to 38 for random inputs: more degenerate, as expected.
- **Cross-model transfer, partial and stronger within the Pythia family:** **the against clause is triggered across families, and in a direction the pre-registration did not name.** Within Pythia the transfer holds: 1B's chain scores -55.7 under 160M against a -31.2 random baseline. Across families it reverses: every model's least-likely text is *more* probable than random text under every other family (SmolLM2's chain: -9.2, -8.7, -7.5 under the others against baselines near -13 and -31; GPT-2's: -16.0, -9.1, -9.1). Two reasons the data support. First, re-encoding destroys the pathology even under the source model: Pythia-1B's chain scores -21.6 as tokens and -13.3 once decoded and re-encoded, back at the random baseline. Least-likeliness lives at the token level, not the text level. Second, uniform random tokens are a very low bar: an anti-greedy chain is made of real subword fragments, and another tokeniser breaks them into common pieces more predictable than tokens drawn at random. The cross-model probe in Q9, as a least-likely-text probe, is dead; a token-level probe works only within a shared tokeniser.

## What this sets

- Minimum-likelihood sequences are a property of a tokeniser and a model together, and do not survive decoding. Drop the cross-family version. Keep the within-family version for lineage work (Pythia sizes; checkpoints), where the shared tokeniser makes it meaningful.
- The random-token input is the usable white-noise substitute, but its continuation must be scored by a text-likeness measure as well as log-probability; the two disagree in about a third of cases.
- The anti-greedy chain is also an under-trained-token detector, which was not its purpose; Pythia-160M's whitespace result belongs to that literature (Land and Bartolo), not to this question.
- Nothing here bears on self-models or endogeneity.
