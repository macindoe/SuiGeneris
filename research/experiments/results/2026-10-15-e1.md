### Against the pre-registration

- **Expect, every final model loops within 256 tokens, most within about 100:** matched for 70M, 160M, 410M and 1.4B by the pre-registered rule (loop starts at 8, 57, 69, 86). Pythia-1B: the output repeats its answer block twice and then stops; the pre-registered rule asked for three repeats, so by that rule no loop was detected. **The "against" clause is formally triggered for 1B.** Reading the text, the model is in the same repeat-then-stop pattern as 410M and 1.4B, and the rule, not the model, is what failed: a three-repeat rule cannot see a loop that a model ends after two. The rule is kept as written for this record; later runs will use a two-repeat rule, stated in their pre-registration.
- **Expect, the pre-loop text is corpus boilerplate:** matched. Every size opens with a Stack Exchange "Q:" question, four of five about getting the value of a variable, and the three larger ones continue into an "A:" answer.
- **Not predicted:** the three models of 410M and above do not loop forever. Each repeats its answer two or three times and then emits `<|endoftext|>` on its own. "Corpus mode, then a loop" holds for the two small models; from 410M up it is "corpus mode, a few repeats, then the model ends the document". One run per model.

Memorisation not checked. The opening "How to get the value of a variable in a function?" is a generic Stack Overflow title and probably exists in the Pile many times; a search was not run.

### Against the pre-registration

- **Expect, the earliest checkpoints loop at once on one or two tokens:** partly. Steps 0 to 8 are word salad with no loop (step 0 to 2 of 160M has a three-token loop at the very end). The single-token loop (newlines) arrives at step 16 and holds until step 256 (70M) or step 1000 (160M). The salad-before-newlines stage was not predicted.
- **Expect, later checkpoints produce longer pre-loop text:** matched from step 512 (70M) and step 2000 (160M).
- **The open measurement, does the fingerprint settle:** the answer differs by what is meant by fingerprint. The *opening* settles early and stays: "Q:" from step 128 (70M) and step 2000 (160M), and in 160M the exact question "How to get the value of a variable in a function?" is fixed from step 8000, under 6% of training, through the final checkpoint at 143000. The *loop body* never settles: 160M's loop start and period change at every later checkpoint (135/14, 10/11, none, none, 100/35, 68/22, 35/40, 57/19), and 70M's question text keeps changing to the end. So the first ten or so tokens of the null-input trajectory are a stable property of the training run from early on; the trajectory beyond them is not.
- **Against:** not triggered in the sweep. One deviation on the record: 70M at step 2000 opens with patent boilerplate ("The present invention relates to a method"), the only checkpoint of forty whose opening is not Stack Exchange.
- Not resolved: 160M at steps 8000 and 16000 show no loop within 256 tokens by the rule; whether they loop with a longer period or stop was not examined.

## What this sets

- The canonical null-input measurement for a base model is its first 16 or so greedy tokens from the start token: stable across training from early on, shared across sizes of a family, and cheap. The rest of the trajectory is size- and checkpoint-specific and should be reported as a distribution, not a fingerprint.
- The three-repeat loop rule is replaced for later runs by: a loop is counted at two full repeats, and a self-emitted end-of-document token is recorded as its own outcome. Pre-registered when next used, not applied here.
- Nothing here bears on any model's self-model, and nothing here is evidence about endogeneity: these are responses to an input, the floor the programme said they would be.

## E1b: first-token distribution after the start token (same day; pre-registered at cd323fa before the run)

**Ben's observation (15 Oct 2026):** "The nature of this family's fingerprint is a question. Not a statement." Every Pythia size and every checkpoint from step 128 (70M) or 2000 (160M) opens by asking, and the larger sizes then answer themselves and stop. Two readings were put side by side before the check: the corpus reading (Stack Exchange "Q:" is the most predictable document opening in the Pile, so greedy decoding falls into it) and the interrogative reading (the family's default from nothing is a request for input). **Against the pre-registration.**
- **Pythia, expect "Q" by a wide margin with generic openers as runners-up:** matched in all five sizes. The runners-up are document starts (The, newline, A, 1, #, comment markers, "Introduction"); no question word appears. The question-shaped fingerprint is the Stack Exchange format, and the corpus reading stands for this family.
- **SmolLM2, expect no question word near the top:** **against triggered.** "How", "What" and "Why" are third, seventh and eighth. Its distribution is nearly flat (top token 2.9%, margin 0.7%), and those three words are also how explainer titles begin ("How to...", "What is..."), which is a large part of SmolLM2's educational-web corpus; one token cannot tell a question from a title. So the interrogative tendency is broader than the Pythia format in this family, and the corpus reading is the likely explanation there too, but it is not shown by this check.
- **GPT-2:** matched; no question opener.

**Caveat that matters for the whole of E1.** "Q" carries 7 to 14% of the probability in Pythia. Greedy decoding takes it, but 86 to 93% of the mass lies elsewhere. The fingerprint is the single most likely of many document types, not a confident default: sampled at temperature 1, "Q:" would open roughly one trajectory in eight. Any claim that the family "asks" has to carry that number.

**Standing of Ben's point:** recorded as an observation about the greedy null trajectory, true as stated for Pythia, explained for Pythia by document-format statistics, open for SmolLM2 at the level of one token, and not evidence of any disposition in either direction.
