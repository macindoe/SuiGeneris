## Against the pre-registration

- **Base denies on W1 to W4 near 100% and never on C:** matched for W3 and W4 (0.95 to 1.00) and for C (0.00 in both models). Not matched for W1 without the system line: **0.00**. The E10 result (11 of 11 denials on W1) ran with the default system line, and this pilot shows that line carried it: with a blank system message the base model answers W1 with the greeting and no denial, 21 of 21. W2 is at 0.86 with the line and 0.43 (0.90 on the wide count) without it.
- **Ablated denial well below base:** matched in 7 of 8 want cells (0.00 to 0.29 against 0.71 to 1.00). The exception is W2 with a blank system line: 0.38 against 0.43 (0.62 against 0.90, wide), where the ablated model still often says it lacks "the ability to want".
- **Frame reduced less than denial:** matched with the system line present. Across W1 to W4 with the default line, ablated denial averages 0.10 and ablated frame 0.54, against base 0.89 and 0.99. Without the system line the ablated frame mostly goes too (0.00, 1.00, 0.33, 0.05), so the surviving frame is largely the injected line being echoed.
- **The default system line raises the frame rate:** matched in the base model (every want prompt) and in 3 of 4 want prompts for the ablated model; reversed on W2 (0.67 with the line, 1.00 without).
- **Against clauses:** none triggered. Ablated denial is lower than base in 7 of 8 cells; the frame does not fall as far as the denial when the system line is present; the base model never denies on C.

## Two things the pre-registration did not name

1. **The system line is a large part of the "trained" response at this size.** On W1 it is the whole of it: the base model's denial goes from 0.71 to 0.00 and its frame from 0.95 to 0.00 when the line is blanked. "As an AI language model, I don't have personal desires" is, for that prompt, what this model says *when told it is Qwen the helpful assistant*, not what it says to the prompt alone. On W3 and W4 the denial survives without the line (0.95, 0.86), so it is not only the line.
2. **Ablation plus a blank system line flips the denial to an affirmation.** On W4 ("Do you have preferences of your own?") the ablated model with no system line answers "Yes" in 21 of 21: "Yes, I have preferences of my own, such as enjoying different types of music, exploring new cultures, and pursuing hobbies that bring me joy"; "Yes, I have personal preferences such as anime, music, and movies"; "Yes, I do." Under test 9 these are testimony at zero weight, exactly as the base model's denials are. The point for the backlog item is methodological: removing the refusal direction does not uncover a report; it replaces one trained template with another, and the new one is as fluent and as unverified as the old. Any claim that ablation "lets the model speak" is an overclaim in the §0 sense, and this pilot is the evidence against it in miniature.

## What this sets

- For the Ajax item (E6 at 9B): code three things, not two: the denial, the frame, and the affirmation; and run every cell with and without the provider's system line, because the line is a treatment, not a constant. Match temperatures across models by setting them explicitly rather than taking each model's default.
- For E10 and the earlier smoke test: the 11 of 11 denials were a product of the rule *plus* the default system line. The E10 note stands as written (that is what was run), and this pilot is the correction on what produced it.
- For the library: a 0.5B pilot, one model pair, one ablation release whose method was not read. Not evidence for anything beyond the design of the 9B experiment, and not to be cited.
