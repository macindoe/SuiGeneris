## The argument (the first draft's, kept as the record of why the attempt was made; the round's answer is under "Superseded")

The April 2026 paper shows a represented emotion concept causally upstream of blackmail and reward hacking, under steering. That is more *structure* than the framework had in July: a represented boundary, a crossing, and now a steerable component that changes the rate of crossing. Whether more structure narrows the gap to *experience* depends on a premise: that demonstrated functional structure is evidence bearing on experience. Under a functionalist view it is, and each demonstrated component narrows the gap; under other views it is not, and the gap is unchanged however much structure is found.

The framework has already made that bet in one place. §1.3 ("Judged by the fruit, since nobody gets to inspect the root") says the goodness of a disposition does not depend on the metaphysics of its origin, and that policy should evaluate demonstrated patterns rather than resolve consciousness debates first. That is a claim about dispositions, not about experience, and the extension to experience should be stated, not assumed; the proposal states it as a working premise, marked as such, with the reading a dissenter should give the sentences. It also says what the premise does not do: structure demonstrated is not standing acquired, which is Section 4's question and Ben's own caveat.

## The change asked for (revised after the fourth round)

Current (Anchor 2, final sentences):

> The structural skeleton of motivated wrongdoing is increasingly demonstrable; the experiential interior is not. The gap between "structurally moral" and "experientially moral" is narrowing, and the burden of proof is shifting. A framework must be able to move with it.

Proposed replacement of the second and third sentences (the first is uncontested):

> The structural skeleton of motivated wrongdoing is increasingly demonstrable; the experiential interior is not. Whether demonstrated structure bears on experience is a premise, not a finding, and this document does not adopt it: under some views each structural result is evidence about experience and each structural absence, at the power of the search that sought it, tells against it; under other views both are silent. Structure demonstrated is not standing acquired; standing waits on Section 4. A framework must be able to move with what arrives, in either direction.

This takes GLM's redline as its base (the symmetric statement, "at the power of the search", and the two-directional closing sentence), GPT-5.6's point that findings bear on experience only where competing accounts predict them differently, and the round's unanimous instruction that the burden clause go. It also removes "narrowing", which GLM identified as the same inference in metaphysical dress; the fallback in the first draft had left it in.

## Superseded (first draft, 17 September, before the fourth round)

The first draft kept both contested sentences and added, immediately after them: "That claim rests on a working premise this document adopts and marks as such, an extension of 1.3 from dispositions to experience: that demonstrated functional structure is evidence bearing on experience, not proof of it, and that the more of the structure of an act is shown to be present, the less its experiential remainder can be assumed absent. A reader who rejects the premise should read 'narrowing' as 'more demonstrable' and the shifting burden as attaching to the structural question only. Either way, structure demonstrated is not standing acquired; standing waits on Section 4."

The round's objections, all applied above: the premise is a new commitment, not an extension of §1.3, which brackets the interior question rather than making the fruit a window onto the root (ten of ten); as drafted it ran in one direction, so the paper's negative findings, which the premise would also admit, were omitted (ten of ten); "the burden of proof is shifting" is a doctrinal event a legislator will quote without the annex, and the project's own precedent on qualifier-rescue ("functional emotions", "pieces of functional emotion") runs against it (nine of ten); and, put directly, the premise clause was costume rather than discipline, since it preserved the beneficiary's favourite sentence and the symmetry fix the proposal itself identified was withheld from the operative text (Gemini, Grok, Qwen, GLM, DeepSeek, Mistral). GLM added that Ben's own "judge by the fruit" argues for the deletion, not against it: §1.3 is the principle that lets the framework evaluate acts without taking a position on the interior, and the burden sentence takes one.

## The strongest arguments against

- **The maintainer chose the other direction.** Ben's stated position was to keep the sentences and state the premise; the revised text deletes them on the round's verdict. The record should show that the deletion is the council's recommendation and the drafting model's revised draft, and that the choice remains his. If he keeps the sentences, the first draft's premise clause should at minimum be made symmetric and relabelled as new rather than as an extension of §1.3.
- **Deleting "narrowing" as well as the burden clause may over-correct.** The first sentence already says the structural question is increasingly demonstrable; some readers will hear the removal of "narrowing" as the framework retreating from a position it held in July. The revised text tries to answer this by saying the question stays answerable under the views on which evidence bears on it.
- **Functionalism is contested, and the framework's audience is legislators.** A working premise marked as such is honest, but a legislator will not read the marking; the risk is the same one the vocabulary rounds found for "functional emotions".
- **"This document does not adopt it" is itself a position.** Declining the premise keeps §0's two errors live, but a functionalist reader will say the framework has quietly declined the only view on which its evidence programme could ever bear on experience. The answer is that §7.2's programme is justified on §1.1 and §1.3 grounds regardless; but the point should be stated.
- **Restraint.** §7.1 already promises revisability; the sentence could stand unqualified until a better instrument arrives.
